What happened. I pulled IP's FY2025 10-K to look for the debt that net debt leaves out. Pensions: the U.S. qualified plan holds $8,482M against $8,116M owed, and all plans together are $144M overfunded. I count the $342M in plans that are underfunded and $133M of retiree medical, after tax, and give no credit for the surplus. Leases: the $707M of operating leases are already inside EBITDA, and the $71M of finance leases are already in debt. Then the contractual obligations footnote: a $487M deferred tax on the Temple-Inland timber notes, due when the notes mature in 2027, against $222M of equity left in the structure. Together, $626M after tax: $1.18 a share.

What I expected. A pension hole; paper companies usually have one. This one doesn't. The surprise was the tax.

What changed in the model. A debt-like line on Inputs, built from the 10-K lines and taken off the equity value, not the WACC weights, since none of it is financing. Mid-case $40.25 → $39.07; P(value > price) 65% → 63%; the odds the price implies 48% → 45%. Two new tie-out checks, 21 in all, all pass. The post's numbers are now generated from the model, so they moved with it.

My P(down). Unchanged at 30%. The 10-K is about the balance sheet, not the cycle, and this log's rule is that the number moves only on evidence about the cycle. The edge narrows from 18 points to 15 points because the company is worth a little less in every scenario.

Not counted, on purpose. $344M of unrecognized tax benefits (timing unknown) and a Brazilian tax assessment shared with Sylvamo, IP's potential share about $274M (a contingency, not booked). Together they'd be another $0.65 and $0.52 a share if they landed in full.

What I believe and why, in one paragraph: the 2025 closures (3.9 Mt, ~10% of North American capacity) were cost economics — the low performers went — so they're permanent, which supports price, but they weren't a demand squeeze, so the tightness is thinner than the headline. Of the $170/ton the index has recognized this year, about half sticks at the box level, with a lag of months; that's $590M of 2027 EBITDA net of what's already in guidance. EMEA is losing money on the paper-to-box lag and is for sale or spin. The dividend isn't covered by cash flow. Mid-cycle NA margin 17%, between SW and PKG. All of that nets to $40 a share on three methods, and a 65% chance the shares are worth more than the price — if the down-cycle probability is 30%. If it's 50%, there's no edge.
